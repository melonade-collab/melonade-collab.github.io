Interactive Prototype

Prioritization Matrix + Roadmap

A four-step flow: score features on the impact/effort matrix, rate reach and confidence for a RICE score, review and confirm your prioritization, then generate the roadmap.

Problem

▼

Feature valuation, prioritization, and delivery planning are treated as three separate activities — a scoring spreadsheet, a priority board, and a roadmap slide that someone updates manually after the other two are done. Each handoff introduces drift: the roadmap stops reflecting the scoring, the scoring stops reflecting what's actually shipping. This prototype collapses all three into one connected flow, where the act of scoring a feature is also the act of prioritizing it, and the roadmap builds itself from that output.

Tech

▼
  • Four-step flow: matrix scoring → RICE rating → Now/Next/Later prioritization review → annual roadmap generation
  • Native HTML5 drag-and-drop for matrix and board; no library dependency
  • RICE score calculated per card from matrix position (Impact + Effort) plus two user inputs (Reach + Confidence); cards sorted by score before roadmap is built
  • Priority badges auto-assigned from RICE ranking: top third = High → Now, middle = Medium → Next, bottom = Low → Later
  • Board allows free re-drag to override any RICE-generated placement
  • Annual roadmap is effort-aware: quadrant placement determines effort level, and high-effort items (Big Bets, Time Sinks) automatically span two consecutive quarters with a continuation card rendered in the second
  • Long-press touch drag with haptic feedback works across matrix and board
  • Back navigation at each step restores the previous state; full reset returns to Step 1 with all cards and scores cleared

Features

▼
  • Step 1 — Score: Drag features from the backlog tray onto the 2×2 matrix (Quick Wins, Big Bets, Fill-ins, Time Sinks)
  • Step 2 — Rate: For each placed feature, select Reach (Niche / Some / Many / Most) and Confidence (Low / Med / High); RICE score calculates live from these inputs combined with the matrix-derived Impact and Effort
  • Step 3 — Prioritize: Now/Next/Later board auto-populates sorted by RICE score with priority badges set; cards can be freely re-dragged to override before committing
  • Step 4 — Roadmap: Annual view distributes items across four quarters; high-effort items (Big Bets, Time Sinks) automatically span two quarters with a continuation card in the following quarter
  • "← Back" navigation at each step restores the previous state with all inputs and placements intact

Why This

▼

The four steps map directly to how prioritization actually works: the matrix surfaces the qualitative call (is this high-impact, low-effort?), RICE adds the quantitative layer that differentiates features that look similar on the matrix, the Now/Next/Later board is where you apply judgment and override what the numbers got wrong, and the annual roadmap is where delivery planning happens. Keeping these in sequence — rather than as separate artifacts — means the roadmap always reflects current scoring, and any override is a deliberate decision made in context rather than a manual edit made days later. The roadmap is also effort-aware: because effort is already encoded in the matrix quadrant, Big Bets and Time Sinks automatically span two quarters — a more honest representation of what delivery actually looks like than placing every item in a single quarter regardless of size.
